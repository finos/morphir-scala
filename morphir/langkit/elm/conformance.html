Elm langkit — conformance with elm/compiler

The standard is elm/compiler: what it accepts, we accept; what it rejects, we reject. Where a caller could reasonably want something else, it is a field on ElmParseOptions that defaults to Elm's behaviour — never a quiet allowance in the parser.

How this document is kept honest.

Every divergence below has a row in the gap ledger and, wherever it can be written as “this valid Elm does not parse”, an assertion pinning that behaviour in a KnownGapsSpec. Closing a gap therefore fails the suite: the pin says the construct is rejected and it no longer is. A gap cannot be closed without deleting its row and its pin in the same commit, and cannot be forgotten while the row is still here.

Two findings that shaped the design

Elm parses operator chains flat (Src.Binops) and resolves precedence during canonicalisation. The parse-then-reassociate split here matches upstream rather than working around it.

Elm's message for an unresolvable chain is “You cannot mix (op1) and (op2) without parentheses.” The conflict is equal precedence with either non-associativity or differing associativity — not merely a non-associative operator used twice.
